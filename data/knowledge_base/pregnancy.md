# Pregnancy Care and Maternal Health Guidelines

**Source**: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) & World Health Organization (WHO)  
**Topic**: Pregnancy & Maternal Health  
**Tier**: TIER_1_PRIMARY_AUTHORITY  
**URL**: https://www.cdc.gov/pregnancy/index.html  

## Early Signs and Diagnosis of Pregnancy
Common early physiological indications of pregnancy include:
- Missed menstrual period in individuals with regular cycles.
- Mild breast tenderness and swelling due to increased estrogen and progesterone.
- Mild nausea with or without vomiting ("morning sickness"), occurring at any time of day.
- Increased urinary frequency and fatigue.
- Definitive diagnosis is confirmed via urine/serum human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) testing and obstetric ultrasound.

## Essential Prenatal Nutrition and Care
- **Folic Acid**: Daily supplementation with 400 mcg of folic acid prior to conception and through the first trimester significantly reduces the risk of neural tube defects.
- **Iron and Calcium**: Adequate dietary intake of iron prevents maternal anemia, while calcium supports fetal skeletal development.
- **Hydration**: Pregnant individuals need at least 8 to 10 glasses of water daily to support amniotic fluid volume and increased maternal blood volume.

## Obstetric Emergency Signs (Seek Urgent Care Immediately)
The following symptoms require immediate emergency clinical evaluation:
- **Vaginal Bleeding**: Any bright red vaginal bleeding during pregnancy, with or without pain.
- **Severe Abdominal Pain**: Sharp, localized abdominal pain or cramping, particularly unilateral pain (which may indicate ectopic pregnancy in early gestation).
- **Severe Headache or Vision Changes**: Persistent severe headache, visual spots, or sudden severe swelling of the hands/face (potential signs of preeclampsia).
- **Reduced Fetal Movement**: Noticeable decrease in fetal movement in the third trimester.
