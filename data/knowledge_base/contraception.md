# Contraceptive Methods and Reproductive Autonomy

**Source**: World Health Organization (WHO) & Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)  
**Topic**: Contraception  
**Tier**: TIER_1_PRIMARY_AUTHORITY  
**URL**: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/family-planning-contraception  

## Categories of Contraceptive Methods

### 1. Barrier Methods
- **Male and Female Condoms**: Only methods that offer dual protection against unintended pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections (STIs/HIV). Typical use effectiveness is approximately 87% (male condoms).

### 2. Short-Acting Hormonal Methods
- **Combined Oral Contraceptive Pills (COCs)**: Contain synthetic estrogen and progestin; work primarily by suppressing ovulation and thickening cervical mucus. Over 99% effective with perfect use (~93% typical use).
- **Progestin-Only Pills (POPs / Mini-pill)**: Suitable for individuals with estrogen contraindications or breastfeeding mothers.

### 3. Long-Acting Reversible Contraception (LARC)
- **Intrauterine Devices (IUDs)**: Copper (non-hormonal) or levonorgestrel-releasing (hormonal). Greater than 99% effectiveness for 3 to 10 years depending on device.
- **Subdermal Implants**: Single-rod progestin implant providing over 99% efficacy for up to 3–5 years.

### 4. Emergency Contraception (EC)
- **Emergency Contraceptive Pills (ECPs)**: Levonorgestrel or Ulipristal acetate pills taken as soon as possible after unprotected intercourse (effective within 72–120 hours). ECPs delay ovulation; they do not terminate an established pregnancy and are not abortifacients.
