# Menstrual Health and Dysmenorrhea Guidelines

**Source**: World Health Organization (WHO) & National Health Service (NHS) Clinical Overview  
**Topic**: Menstrual Health

## The Normal Menstrual Cycle
The menstrual cycle is a regular natural change that occurs in the female reproductive system that makes pregnancy possible. A typical menstrual cycle lasts between 21 and 35 days, with an average of 28 days. Menstruation (bleeding) typically lasts between 3 and 7 days.

### Key Phases of the Cycle
1. **Menstrual Phase (Days 1–5)**: The uterine lining (endometrium) is shed when pregnancy does not occur.
2. **Follicular Phase (Days 1–13)**: Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) stimulates ovarian follicles to mature.
3. **Ovulation (Day 14)**: Luteinizing hormone (LH) surge triggers the release of a mature egg from the ovary.
4. **Luteal Phase (Days 15–28)**: Progesterone is produced by the corpus luteum to support potential implantation.

## Understanding Menstrual Cramps (Dysmenorrhea)
Primary dysmenorrhea refers to recurrent, crampy lower abdominal pain occurring with menses in the absence of identifiable pelvic pathology. It is caused by the release of prostaglandins in the endometrium during menstruation, causing uterine muscle contractions and temporary vasoconstriction.

### Evidence-Based Relief Measures
- **Heat Therapy**: Applying a heating pad or warm water bottle (approximately 40°C) to the lower abdomen is as effective as standard over-the-counter analgesics.
- **Hydration & Nutrition**: Adequate water intake reduces bloating and fluid retention. Magnesium and Vitamin B1 supplementation have shown mild benefit in clinical studies.
- **Gentle Physical Activity**: Light aerobic exercise releases endorphins, which act as natural pain modulators.

## When to Seek Immediate Medical Evaluation (Red Flags)
While mild to moderate cramping is common, individuals should consult a healthcare provider if they experience:
- Bleeding that soaks through one or more sanitary pads or tampons every hour for two consecutive hours.
- Severe, debilitating pain that does not respond to standard pain relief or disrupts daily activities.
- Pelvic pain accompanied by sudden high fever, foul-smelling vaginal discharge, or severe dizziness/fainting.
