# Endometriosis Pathophysiology and Clinical Care

**Source**: World Health Organization (WHO) & Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists (RCOG)  
**Topic**: Endometriosis  
**Tier**: TIER_1_PRIMARY_AUTHORITY  
**URL**: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/endometriosis  

## Definition and Pathophysiology
Endometriosis is a chronic inflammatory condition characterized by the presence of endometrium-like tissue (stroma and glands) outside the uterine cavity, most commonly on the ovaries, fallopian tubes, pelvic peritoneum, bladder, and bowel. This ectopic tissue undergoes cyclical bleeding, inducing local inflammation, fibrosis, and adhesion formation.

## Hallmark Symptoms
- **Severe Dysmenorrhea**: Debilitating period pain that does not resolve with standard analgesia.
- **Chronic Pelvic Pain**: Non-cyclical, continuous lower abdominal and back pain.
- **Dyspareunia**: Deep pain during or after sexual intercourse.
- **Dyschezia and Dysuria**: Painful bowel movements or urination, particularly exacerbated during menses.
- **Subfertility**: Reduced reproductive fecundity due to anatomical distortion or peritoneal inflammation.

## Diagnostic Approach
- **Clinical Evaluation**: Detailed symptom history and specialized bimanual pelvic examination.
- **Imaging**: Transvaginal high-resolution ultrasound (TVUS) or pelvic MRI by an experienced radiologist to identify endometriomas and deep infiltrating nodules.
- **Gold Standard**: Laparoscopy with histological biopsy remains the definitive diagnostic standard.

## Medical and Surgical Management
- **First-Line Medical Therapy**: Combined oral contraceptives or high-dose progestins (such as dienogest) to suppress ovarian estrogen production and induce endometrial atrophy.
- **Surgical Excision**: Conservative laparoscopic excision of endometriotic lesions performed by specialized gynecological surgeons.
