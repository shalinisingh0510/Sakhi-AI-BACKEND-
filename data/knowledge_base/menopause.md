# Menopause and Perimenopause Clinical Overview

## Stages of Menopausal Transition
1. **Perimenopause**: The transitional phase leading up to menopause, typically beginning in the mid-to-late 40s. Characterized by fluctuating estrogen and progesterone levels, leading to cycle irregularities, hot flashes, sleep disturbances, and mood changes.
2. **Menopause**: Medically defined as the permanent cessation of menstruation, diagnosed retrospectively after 12 consecutive months of amenorrhea (absence of periods) without other pathological or physiological causes. Average age is 51 years.
3. **Postmenopause**: The lifespan following menopause.

## Common Vasomotor and Physical Symptoms
- **Hot Flashes and Night Sweats**: Sudden feelings of intense heat, primarily on the face, neck, and chest, caused by hypothalamic thermoregulatory adjustments.
- **Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause (GSM)**: Vaginal dryness, discomfort during intercourse, and increased susceptibility to urinary tract infections due to reduced estrogen.
- **Bone Density Reduction**: Decreased estrogen accelerates bone resorption, increasing osteoporosis risk.

## Evidence-Based Management
- **Lifestyle Adjustments**: Layered clothing, avoiding caffeine/alcohol triggers, keeping sleep environments cool, and regular weight-bearing exercise to protect bone density.
- **Vaginal Moisturizers and Lubricants**: Non-hormonal moisturizers and water- or silicone-based lubricants alleviate vaginal dryness.
- **Menopausal Hormone Therapy (MHT)**: In consultation with a gynecologist, MHT is an effective treatment for moderate-to-severe vasomotor symptoms for eligible candidates without contraindications.
